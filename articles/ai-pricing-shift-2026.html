Pricing

Every major AI tool changed its pricing in 2026. Here's how to actually pick one.

Published September 2026 · 7 min read

Flat, one-price-fits-all subscriptions quietly disappeared from AI tools this year. What replaced them is more accurate to actual usage — and more confusing to shop for. Here's what changed, and a framework for matching a plan to how you actually work instead of guessing.

Between June and July 2026, four of the biggest AI platforms restructured their pricing within weeks of each other. That's not a coincidence — it's what happens when providers realize flat pricing was subsidizing a small minority of heavy users at the expense of everyone else. The industry is moving toward metering because inference is a real, variable cost, and "unlimited" was never sustainable once usage caught up to the hype.

Here's what actually happened:

Notice the pattern: every provider kept (or added) a genuinely cheap entry tier, then split the expensive tier in two so occasional power users and true heavy users stop paying the same price. That's the tell. If you were on a flat "pro" plan and barely used it, you're the customer these changes are designed to catch — you're about to get quietly moved into a cheaper bucket, or you'll keep overpaying if you don't act.

The framework: pick by your actual weekly pattern, not the plan name

Skip the marketing tiers for a second and ask three questions about how you actually use the tool:

1. Are you using it daily for real output, or a few times a week to unblock yourself? Daily, heavy, generation-focused use (long documents, big codebases, agentic workflows) is where the top tiers — ChatGPT Pro $200, Claude Team Premium, Copilot Pro+ — start to pay for themselves. Occasional or exploratory use almost never justifies them; the $6-25/month entry tiers cover most of what a non-power-user actually needs.

2. Is the cost coming out of your own pocket or a business budget? On a personal budget, the calculus is simple: measure what you use in a typical week against the entry-tier limits before paying for headroom you won't touch. On a business budget, the metered/credit model (Copilot's AI Credits, in particular) is actually easier to justify to finance than flat "unlimited" ever was, because you can point to real usage data instead of a guess.

3. Do you need the model, or do you need the integration? This is the one people get wrong most often. If what you value is "GPT-5-class reasoning" in the abstract, the cheapest tier that includes it is usually enough. If what you value is the tool wired into your actual workflow — Copilot in your IDE, Claude in your terminal, ChatGPT with memory and connectors — that's what the higher tiers are really charging you for, and it's worth pricing against the time it saves, not the model quality alone.

What we'd actually do

Start one tier below where you think you belong. Every one of these platforms now has a genuinely usable entry price ($5-25/month) that didn't exist in this form a year ago — the "unlimited or nothing" choice is gone. Run a normal week on the cheap tier, and let real usage — not anxiety about hitting a limit — tell you whether to upgrade. Given how recently these changes landed, expect at least one more round of adjustment within the next two quarters as providers see how usage actually splits across the new tiers.
